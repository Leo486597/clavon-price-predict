  • The fit is real, but flattered. Across all 281 sales the average miss is only ~$17/psf — but that's inflated by tightly-priced launch units. On recent (2024+) sales the typical miss is ~$35/psf and the spread is ±$43/psf. That's the error you should actually expect: roughly ±$33k on this unit.
  • The data is lopsided toward the past. Of the 281 peer sales, 214 are launch "New" sales (2020–21) and 62 are pre-completion sub-sales — only 5 are true resales. Resale, sub-sale and launch markets don't price identically, so the model leans on a lot of older, structurally-cheaper evidence.
  • The very-recent window is sparse. Just 5 of the 764 sqft sales occurred in 2026, and only 9 stack-16 BP3 sales since 2024 (ranging $2,057–$2,198 psf, a real $140 spread). Small samples ⇒ wider uncertainty on "today's" price.
  • One near-perfect anchor saves it. #22-16 is the same stack, same BP3 layout, three floors up, sold just two months ago. Having that single high-quality comparable is what lifts confidence from "model guess" to "defensible".

Strengths & weaknesses

✓ What makes this trustworthy

  • Large, clean sample. 826 transactions; 281 exact-type peers.
  • Like-for-like. Compared only to 764 sqft 2BR Premium units — not the whole project.
  • Layout captured precisely. BP1/2/3 read from the actual floor-plan URLs, so the +$89 BP3 premium is measured, not assumed.
  • High explanatory power. R² = 0.98; floor + time + layout account for nearly all variation.
  • A gold-standard comparable exists (#22-16) and the estimate is tied to it.
  • Transparent & reproducible — one script, no hidden steps.

✗ Where it can be wrong

  • Blind to unit condition. Caveats carry no info on renovation, furnishing, tenancy or actual view quality — a renovated unit can beat the model by tens of thousands.
  • Time-trend extrapolation. The +$8/mo slope is set by launch-era data and over-shoots at the tail (proven: +$59 on #22-16).
  • Thin resale evidence. Only 5 true resales among peers; mostly launch + sub-sale data.
  • View premium is qualitative. The south-facing sea/cruise aspect isn't a model variable — it's argued, not measured.
  • No macro factors. Interest rates, cooling measures, MRT/amenity changes and overall sentiment aren't modelled.
  • Linear assumptions. Floor premium is treated as constant; real markets bend (penthouse jumps, low-floor discounts).
  • Survivorship. Only units that sold appear — unsold listings (and their asking prices) are invisible.

How to make it more accurate

  • Add active listings. Current asking prices for other 764 sqft units would calibrate "today" far better than 2024–25 caveats.
  • Factor in your unit's condition. Recent renovation, furnishing and the real view from #19-16 can justify pushing above the model.
  • Re-run as new caveats land. Each fresh stack-16 / BP3 resale tightens the estimate — the pipeline re-runs from build.py in seconds.
  • Cross-check URA. Verify the scraped caveats against official URA data before listing.
